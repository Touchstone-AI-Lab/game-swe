## Owned rules and exact pricing

There are three ordinary Common rule Jokers: `j_sp2_flat` (cost $4), `j_sp2_ratio` (cost $6), and `j_sp2_coupon` (cost $4). Each physical Flat contributes a permanent $1 reduction while owned. Each physical Ratio contributes a multiplicative factor 2/3 while owned. Each newly acquired Coupon supplies one $2 reduction for the next qualifying successful purchase. Multiple instances are independent; selling one removes only its contribution. A spent Coupon remains owned and cannot regain its allowance through display, reroll, moving to another shop or selling another source. The rule Jokers have no scoring or periodic cash effects.

Eligible offers are ordinary non-rule **Jokers** in the normal shop. These three rule types themselves, consumables, playing cards, vouchers, boosters and reroll fees retain their ordinary native prices. Let B be an offer's undiscounted native price, F the number of owned Flats, R the number of owned Ratios and C the number of owned unspent Coupons. Its price is:

`max(1, floor((B - F - 2*C) * 2^R / 3^R))`.

Subtract all fixed reductions first, multiply all rational factors, round down once at the end, then apply the $1 minimum. Neither a previously discounted quote nor its sale value is a new base price. Repeated viewing/recalculation must neither compound a discount nor consume a Coupon. Each successful qualifying purchase consumes all the Coupons that contributed to that transaction, once; a nonqualifying purchase consumes none.

## Real transactions and lifecycle

Current offers immediately reflect an acquisition, source removal or allowance consumption after that action settles. Offers created by a subsequent native reroll or later normal shop use the current rules. UI quotation and actual purchase debit must agree. Preserve the native bought physical object, remaining offers and unrelated objects; no replacement, duplication, extra reward or refund. Selling gives the normal native sale value and only removes that object's own rule, if any.

Insufficient funds or full native destination capacity must reject before ownership, money, sources or allowances change. A later legitimate sale can make the same offer purchasable under the same rules. The fixed buy RPC expresses these refusals as JSON-RPC `BAD_REQUEST` (-32001); a transport failure or arbitrary error is not a successful refusal. Never require a repeated unconfirmed purchase.

The native purchase context runs after acquisition but before its money debit. A committed transaction must retain the price agreed for that purchased object through the native debit; update remaining offers without corrupting the in-flight sale. Core protocol quote validation, versioning and idempotent receipts are specified independently in API.md. Core-only abnormal requests are not claims about event sequences generated by the engine.
