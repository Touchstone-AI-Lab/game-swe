Guardian players report that queued Stasis operations can bring back an already removed orb, create extra orb slots, or exhaust the same volatile card twice. A card selected for Stasis can remain in its old pile while an orb holds that exact card, or be held by two orbs. Generating several one-cost cards at once can push an existing Stasis card out even though Stasis protects cards already stored there.

Repair this physical-card transfer lifecycle. A conditional return should affect the requested orb only while it is still channelled; stale or duplicate requests should have no further effect. Keep the hand-room check for a live conditional return, native orb-slot capacity, ordinary return, and one physical exhaustion per volatile card. Multiple evokes should still trigger the card's per-evoke effects each time, including custom cards, while physically returning or exhausting its stored card just once.

Placement from a specified pile should claim the exact card only while that pile owns it. A card already held in Stasis must not be claimed again. An abandoned placement should restore its temporary retain change to the previous value, while leaving the state of a card already claimed by another Stasis operation alone. Preserve successful hand/discard placement, previously retained cards on rejection, and placement of newly generated cards without a source pile.

Requesting zero random draw-pile cards should leave the piles and Stasis contents alone. Keep normal multi-card placement and native discard-to-draw reshuffling. Batch generation should check the capacity available as its queued transfers execute: keep existing Stasis cards, replace an ordinary orb when that is the available slot, and admit no more cards once every slot holds Stasis. An overflow generated card should not be forced into the hand. Ordinary one/two-card generation and rejection when Stasis is full should keep working.

Recursion also exposes the ownership problem: after evoking and rechanneling a stored card, the very same physical card can be in the hand or exhaust pile and still be held by the orb. The returned/exhausted card should keep its proper destination; the rechannelled Stasis should hold a distinct card with equivalent stats and upgrades and preserve the remaining countdown. The original card must not receive another entry notification. Ordinary non-Stasis orbs should keep their native behavior. No particular orb identity, copying API, channel hook, queue strategy or storage representation is required.

Preserve public APIs and the engine's action queue. The full project is in `/app/downfall`; run `bash build.sh` to compile all source offline.
